How to print at home

How to print at home

  • Download the free PDF and open it.
  • Choose Print, then pick 'Fit to page' (or 'Scale to fit') on A4 or Letter paper.
  • Print on one side only, then glue the pages back to back with a glue stick and bind them however you like (see the binding tips below).
  • The inside pages are black and white, so they're ready for colouring in.

Book pages are square, 216 x 216 mm.

Binding tips

Binding tips and tricks

Works for any Mr. Pepo book. Print one side, glue the pages back to back, then bind however you like.

First: glue the pages back to back

  • Print one side only, and keep the pages in order.
  • Glue each pair back to back so both pictures face out. Smooth from the middle.
  • Dry under a book, then bind it any way you like.

Staple gun

  • Put cardboard underneath so the staple doesn't mark the table.
  • Staple through the middle, pull the cardboard off, and bend the ends flat.

Needle and thread

  • An adult helps with the needle.
  • Make the holes first, then sew. Several sheets at once is fiddly, but it works.

Bent wire (paper clip or garden wire)

  • An adult helps. Wire ends are sharp.
  • Push a straight piece through the middle and bend it tight around the edges.

Any way you bind it is the right way. Have fun with it.

Neurodiversity awareness

What if a neurotypical person had to live a neurodiverse day?

Your radio starts at a random volume, and you have to guess it before you can even think. The grocery aisles move every week, and nobody gives you a map. People ask open questions like 'How are you?' with no options — but they want the long answer. Your boss says 'circle back,' and they mean today.

The bright hallway lights feel like a spotlight. Small talk has invisible rules, and you only learn them by getting it wrong. A deadline sounds soft… until suddenly it isn't. You rehearse a phone call three times, and you still freeze.

It's not bad. It's just different.
And different comes with huge advantages too.

I have super quick reflexes. I'm able to see many things what many people miss. I process things fast, and I catch inconsistencies others walk right past. I have the real talent to do stuff with my hands — paper craft, crochet, building things.

Whenever some door gets closed in front of me, I'm that kind who will be obsessed to look for another door to open. I cannot sit still and accept — some people call it fate. I solve whatever situation I'm in. That drive is mine, and I own it.

If people got hurt by me in the past, or ever do in the future, I apologize — honestly, not from guilt. Being neurodiverse is different, and it is good. Once you learn to control certain aspects, those traits become almost like superpowers.

The world is built for the neurotypical default, so simple tasks can become big obstacles. If this helps you understand a little better, I'd love to hear your thoughts.

What Pepo Studios is

Pepo Studios is my project. Mr. Pepo is the guy with the light bulb: quirky, witty and full of ideas, because he is neurodiverse. I turn his ideas into printable paper, colouring sheets and bedtime books.

Pepo Studios has applied for a Create NSW grant to bring Mr. Pepo and the Paper Sky to families.

For people like me, the overwhelm shows up in deep focus. If someone rattles me then, it can feel like slamming on the brakes.

The cause

It is a neurodiversity awareness project by Pepo Studios. Different brains build brilliant things, and every page you print helps more people see that. No big company, just a lot of paper, glue and ideas.

Why I'm doing this

I'm doing this because I finally saw what I've been living with all my life, and how little awareness there is around us.

People with this special ability are often suffering, even while some of them do incredible things. These minds can build something brilliant, or they can be pushed onto a hard path. That's what I want people to see.

— Peter

Moving forward

I'm fed up with how the system treats people like me. I'm not bitter, and I'm not asking for pity. I'm turning that into strength and pushing forward.

I want this to reach as many people as possible, in Australia and around the world. Free for everyone, or support what it's worth.

— Peter

Limited to 20 members per class.

How to get in

For people with a special ability who are building something real and want to help others too. If that's you, apply.

  1. Apply with your name, email, and your special ability or what you want to build.
  2. Answer a few questions.
  3. Peter reviews every application personally.
  4. Approved members get the private page and access code by email.

Approved members share a private area for business ideas, visible only with the access code. Other groups can be hosted under joint supervision. Tell me straight what's working.

Weekly sessions

One-hour sessions once a week, on a Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday that suits the group. We talk through how a plan can happen. I don't spend time on why it can't.

Alpha Mastermind is full (20 members). Apply anyway and Peter will place you in another group or the next opening.

Free Halloween window ghost

A friendly floating ghost for your window. Free to download and print at home.

  1. Download the ghost PDF.
  2. Take a plain A4 sheet and put three straight stripes of double-sided tape on it.
  3. Stick a big sheet of baking paper onto the taped A4.
  4. Trim the edges to A4 with scissors, or with a guillotine if you have one (a guillotine is easier).
  5. Print the ghost onto the baking paper side.
  6. Put it on the window.
